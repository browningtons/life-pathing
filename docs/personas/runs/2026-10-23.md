# Persona Walkthrough — 2026-10-23

*Ten likely customers, ten fresh-context agents, one production build each. Question:
what stops a stranger from paying $19 for the Reading, and what would the first screen
and the path to the purchase have to be for them to pay?*

**Build under test:** repo HEAD a280de3 (2026-10-22) · origin/main a280de3 · build 2026-10-24T02:08, `VITE_READING_ENABLED=1`. The built bundle's hash (`index-D82ciR-Z.js`) matched production's `index.html`, so the panel tested exactly what was live. Nothing landed on `main` between the walk and the checks, so the re-check was done by the coordinator against the source and the same build (the factual claims below are each marked verified or harness).

**Angle:** Paul's instruction: "I don't necessarily want more features added, I want customers. If we need to completely re-imagine this landing page and conversion to paid, let's do it." The standing roster was reused verbatim (cards still say $29; the brief told them to read $19) so the scoreboard compares with 2026-10-14. The brief added three questions: where would a real you have left, what single change flips you, and what should the first screen say.

**Cost:** ten Sonnet walkers at 92–111k tokens each (about 1.03M); two fix agents (one Opus, one Sonnet) with their verifiers, reported below.

**Headline:** 2 of 10 would pay (0 of 10 last run). All ten gave the same first reason, and it is not the Reading: **the first screen sells nothing.** Every persona described it as "a free numerology calculator" showing "someone else's Life Path 5", with no sentence saying what the product is, who it is for, or that anything costs money; the price first appears two or three tabs in. Eight of ten said a real version of them leaves on that screen. Behind it, the second consensus: the locked part gives no taste ("Six more sections" is a list of titles), while the free opening paragraph is the strongest writing in the app and the one thing that moved people toward paying.

**Real traffic, for calibration:** still none. Vercel Web Analytics on the `life-pathing` project answers "Web Analytics not found" (checked 2026-10-23). Nine days after the last run flagged it, every funnel event is still discarded. The panel's "stayed" verdicts say nothing about bounce; this time the brief asked the question outright, and 8 of 10 said they would have bounced or nearly.

## Harness artifacts (not app bugs, not filed)
- **Tom's "changing letters after buying opens any Reading for free."** The harness mock for `/api/reading` returns the text for any key; production's route checks `client_reference_id` and answers 402 "other reading" for a session bound to another pairing (`api/_lib/entitlement.ts`). The app then shows "This purchase is for another pairing". The paid Profile sections do stay open for any letters, by design.
- **Priya's "after paying I landed on Life Path, not the Reading."** `{"buy": true}` reloads with the just-purchased flag already set, so the false→true transition that sends a real buyer to the Reading tab never fires. In production the `#session_id` redirect does fire it.
- Kevin could not test tap-outside on the modal (the overlay click times out in the harness). Unverified, not a finding.
- The 70% dimension bars after typing letters (Sam) are the documented typed-letters convention, labelled on screen.

## Already fixed before this run (don't refile)
- Everything in the 2026-10-14 report's W1–W13: double-taps, Escape, master-number rows, Life Path 0, Restore as a form, Archetypes browsing, the borderline nudge, the letters ask above the fold. Tom and Kevin re-tested the edge cases and double-taps; all pass.

## What worked (keep it)
- **The free opening paragraph.** Nine of ten quoted it as the best writing in the app and the thing that made them curious; Tom called it "the best sales copy in the app". It is currently the last thing a stranger sees, not the first.
- **The modal.** Price at the top, a real excerpt, "30-day refund, no questions. No subscription, no account." Buy above the fold on a phone. Aisha bought in four taps.
- **Honesty lines.** "Anyone who types the same four gets the same numbers", "typed, not scored", "Read it as a journal prompt, not a measurement", the Reset confirm. Rachel: "the app's strongest asset for my profession".
- The quiz on a phone, the paste parser's ignored-lines report, the reduction table, print and offline, restore with an email only.

## Lessons
1. Asking for the bounce verdict directly, and for "where would you have left", turned a friction panel into a conversion panel: the answer was unanimous and it was the first screen, which no previous finding had ranked.
2. Ask every persona for a "pay without thinking" price. Ten numbers give a median; one "$19 feels steep" does not.
3. A per-key paid artifact needs a per-key mock. The harness's new `bodyFile`/`select` mock served every persona the real Reading for their pairing, so eight read the paid text and judged it, which the last run could not do.
4. Two harness artifacts came from the simulated purchase (reload with the flag preset; a mock that ignores session binding). Both were read off the source in two minutes. Keep the rule: when a finding touches the buy transition, read the code before filing.
