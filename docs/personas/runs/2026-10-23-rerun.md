## Harness artifacts (not filed)
- Tom: after buying, a reload lands on the Life Path tab with the "Your Reading is open" banner again. The banner survives a reload by design (session storage); the nav-to-Reading fires once per purchase. The simulated purchase makes this look odd; in production the buyer arrives on the Reading tab.
- Kevin's INFP came from arbitrary quiz taps; he said so.

## What worked (keep it)
- **The first screen now does its job.** Ten of ten understood the offer, the price, "once" and "no account" before scrolling. Nobody left there. Carlos: "What kept me was the price being stated up front."
- **The free opening paragraph** is still the product's best salesperson. Eight of ten quoted it as what moved them.
- **The modal**, the honesty lines, the reduction table, the quiz on a phone, the paste parser, restore by email, no double-buy path. All held.
- **#22's changes landed cleanly:** Sam truth-tested `/privacy` and the Buy link and found them consistent; Dave read "Written for an 8"; Aisha got the restore guidance text.

## Lessons
1. A first-screen change moved the panel from 2 to 7 would-pay in one day with no change to the product or price. The method's "re-run the same roster" step is the measurement; keep doing it after each change rather than guessing.
2. When a panel converges on "show me the real thing", the fix is to move the existing good content earlier, not to write new copy. The opening paragraph existed on 14 October; it took two runs to put it where a stranger would see it.
3. The three holdouts have stable reasons across three runs (ChatGPT, professional framing, phone skimmer). They are a segment, not noise. Decide whether they are the customer before chasing them.
